# H-1B Visa Research Notes
*Sources: ellis.com/visas/h1b, ellis.com/resources/h-1b-visa-everything-you-need-to-know, ellis.com/resources*

---

## Overview

The H-1B is a nonimmigrant work visa allowing U.S. employers to hire foreign professionals in **specialty occupations** — roles that require a bachelor's degree or higher in a specific field. The total process from job offer to first day of work spans roughly **12–18 months**, largely because the lottery runs on an annual federal calendar with a hard October 1st start date.

- **Initial duration:** 3 years, extendable to 6 years total
- **Extensions beyond 6 years:** Only possible with a pending green card application
- **Family:** Spouse and children under 21 can come on H-4 visas
- **Dual intent:** H-1B holders can pursue a green card concurrently
- **Portability:** Can transfer to a new employer once a new petition is filed — no need to wait for approval

---

## The 5 Phases

### Phase 1 — Pre-Lottery (Before March)

#### Milestone 1: Job Offer & Eligibility Check
The employer confirms the role qualifies as a "specialty occupation" under the Code of Federal Regulations — meaning it genuinely requires at least a bachelor's degree in a specific field. Common qualifying roles: software engineers, researchers, scientists, financial analysts, architects. Not all jobs qualify; the employer must be able to demonstrate the requirement in writing.

**What the candidate needs:**
- Signed offer letter
- Verified copies of degrees and transcripts
- Identity documents (passport)

**What the employer needs:**
- Job title and description of duties
- Worksite location
- Salary offer at or above the prevailing wage for that role/location

---

#### Milestone 2: myUSCIS Account Setup
The employer creates an organizational account on myUSCIS — the central portal for lottery registration and all USCIS interactions. Must exist before registration opens. Legal counsel (e.g. Ellis) is added to the account to interact on the employer's behalf.

---

#### Milestone 3: Document Collection
All candidate and employer documents gathered and verified. Critical: details must be **exactly consistent** across every document — USCIS petitions, the LCA, offer letters, and transcripts. Mismatches are a leading cause of Requests for Evidence (RFEs) and delays.

**Documents to collect:**
- Passport (valid at least 6 months beyond intended U.S. entry date)
- Bachelor's degree or higher (or equivalent — degree equivalency evaluation may be needed for non-U.S. degrees)
- Transcripts
- Employment contract
- Job description with duties, location, salary

---

### Phase 2 — Lottery (March)

#### Milestone 4: Electronic Lottery Registration *(~March 7–24 annually)*
Employer registers each sponsored candidate through the H-1B Electronic Registration Process on myUSCIS. This is **not** the full petition — just identification and a $215 fee per candidate. Registration window is open for a minimum of 14 calendar days.

**Annual cap:**
- 65,000 regular H-1B visas
- 20,000 additional for candidates with a U.S. master's degree or higher
- Some nonprofits and higher education employers are cap-exempt (no lottery required)

**Recent lottery statistics:**

| Fiscal Year | Eligible Registrations | Selected |
|---|---|---|
| 2021 | 269,424 | 124,415 |
| 2022 | 301,447 | 131,924 |
| 2023 | 474,421 | 127,600 |
| 2024 | 758,994 | 188,400 |
| 2025 | 470,342 | 135,137 |
| 2026 | 343,981 | 120,141 |

FY2026: roughly **1 in 3 odds** for a single registration.

**Multiple registrations:** Candidates can have more than one employer register them, giving multiple lottery entries. In FY2026, 7,828 beneficiaries had multiple registrations.

---

#### Milestone 5: Lottery Selection Notice
USCIS notifies employers of selected candidates, typically by late March or early April. If not selected, the process ends for that fiscal year. USCIS sometimes conducts a **second lottery draw** if the cap isn't filled in the first round.

---

### Phase 3 — Petition Filing (April–June)

#### Milestone 6: LCA Filing with the Department of Labor *(~7 business days)*
For selected candidates only. Employer files a Labor Condition Application (LCA) with the DOL via the FLAG portal. The LCA certifies:
- The worker will be paid at least the **prevailing wage** for the role and location
- Working conditions won't adversely affect other workers

LCA approval is a legal prerequisite — the I-129 petition cannot be filed without it.

---

#### Milestone 7: Document Preparation *(~3 days with Ellis)*
Ellis (or immigration counsel) drafts the legal brief, completes USCIS Form I-129, compiles the certified LCA, employee credentials, job description, and all supporting exhibits. The legal brief argues why the role qualifies as a specialty occupation and why the candidate is qualified.

---

#### Milestone 8: Attorney Final Review *(~1 day)*
Attorney performs final accuracy and compliance check before filing. This is the last gate before submission — catching issues here is far cheaper than responding to a post-filing RFE.

---

#### Milestone 9: I-129 Petition Filed with USCIS
The complete petition is submitted to USCIS. Two processing options:

| Option | Timeline | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Standard processing | 3–4 months | Included in base fees |
| Premium processing | 15 business days | +$2,805 |

**Filing fees breakdown (employer pays most):**

| Fee | Amount |
|---|---|
| Lottery registration fee | $215 |
| Form I-129 filing fee (varies by company size) | $460 or $780 |
| Fraud prevention and detection fee | $500 |
| ACWIA training fee (varies by company size) | $750 or $1,500 |
| Asylum program fee (varies by company size) | $0, $300, or $600 |
| Premium processing (optional) | $2,805 |
| H-1B visa stamping fee | $205 |
| **New: Restriction on Entry fee (mandatory, one-time)** | **$100,000** |

> **The $100,000 fee** was introduced via White House proclamation effective September 21, 2025. It applies to candidates who are outside the U.S. at petition filing, inside the U.S. but ineligible for change of status, or who leave the U.S. after filing but before approval. This is now the dominant cost of H-1B sponsorship.

**Ellis legal fee:** $3,000 (includes overnight shipping, degree equivalency, consulate interview practice, and all USCIS responses including RFEs and NOIDs)

### Phase 4 — Approval & Entry (Summer–Oct 1)

#### Milestone 10: USCIS Decision — Approval or RFE
Two possible outcomes:
- **Approved:** USCIS issues an I-797 approval notice. Process continues.
- **Request for Evidence (RFE):** USCIS asks for additional documentation. Ellis responds. Unresolved RFEs lead to denial. RFEs are increasingly common and can add 2–4 months.

---

#### Milestone 11: Visa Stamping / Consular Processing *(for overseas candidates)*
The candidate, still outside the U.S., must obtain a visa stamp at their local U.S. embassy or consulate.

**Steps:**
1. File Form DS-160 via the Consular Electronic Application Center (CEAC)
2. Pay the $205 nonimmigrant visa application fee
3. Schedule and attend a nonimmigrant visa interview

**Documents to bring to the interview:**
- Valid passport (6+ months beyond entry date)
- DS-160 confirmation page
- Approved I-129 petition and I-797 approval notice
- Fee payment receipts
- 2 passport-sized photos meeting U.S. State Dept. requirements

Ellis offers **consulate interview practice** with a former consulate officer.

*Note: If the candidate is already in the U.S. on a valid status, USCIS processes a change of status directly — no consular appointment needed.*

---

#### Milestone 12: Travel & U.S. Entry
Candidate travels to the U.S. with their visa stamp. An I-94 record (proof of authorized status) is generated automatically at the port of entry. **Ellis advises checking the I-94 record online after arrival** — errors occur and must be corrected before they cause compliance issues.

---

#### Milestone 13: Employment Begins — October 1st
H-1B status is tied to the federal fiscal year. Work **cannot legally begin before October 1st** regardless of when the petition was approved or when the candidate entered the U.S. This is the hard wall that creates the 12–18 month total timeline for anyone starting the process the previous spring.

---

### Phase 5 — Ongoing Status (Life on H-1B)

#### Milestone 14: Status Maintenance & Renewals
- H-1B is valid for an initial **3-year term**
- Extendable for another **3 years** (6 years total)
- Extensions beyond 6 years require a pending green card application
- If laid off or let go, the holder has a **60-day grace period** to find a new sponsor, change status, or depart
- Employer changes: portability rules allow transfer to a new employer once a new I-129 petition is filed — work can begin immediately upon filing, no need to wait for approval

#### Milestone 15: Green Card Pathway (Optional)
H-1B is dual-intent, meaning pursuing a green card doesn't jeopardize H-1B status. Common pathways from H-1B:
- **EB-2 PERM** — employer-sponsored, requires PERM labor certification
- **EB-2 NIW** — National Interest Waiver, self-petitioned
- **EB-1A** — Extraordinary ability, self-petitioned, no employer required
- **EB-3 PERM** — employer-sponsored, slightly lower bar than EB-2

Priority dates (based on country of birth) determine wait times — Indian and Chinese nationals face multi-year backlogs for EB-2/EB-3.

## Key Risks & Watch-outs

- **Lottery odds:** ~1 in 3 for FY2026. Not selected = start over next March.
- **$100K fee:** As of Sept 2025, mandatory for overseas candidates. Dramatically changes the employer cost calculus.
- **RFEs:** Increasingly common. USCIS can request more evidence at any point post-filing. Having strong documentation upfront is the main mitigation.
- **Specialty occupation scrutiny:** USCIS has been tightening what qualifies. Generic job titles without a clear degree requirement are risky.
- **Degree equivalency:** Non-U.S. degrees may require a formal equivalency evaluation — adds time and cost if not done early.
- **Consular delays:** Interview scheduling varies dramatically by country. Some U.S. embassies have multi-month waits.
- **I-94 errors:** Check online immediately after arrival. Errors in the automated record happen and must be corrected.
- **Layoff/job loss:** 60-day grace period. No time to waste — Ellis recommends acting immediately.

## For the PoC: Recommended Step Data

Given this research, here are the 7 most important milestones to show in the road visualization for an overseas candidate being employer-sponsored:

1. **Job Offer** — Role confirmed as specialty occupation, offer letter signed
2. **Lottery Registration** — Employer registers in March (~$215, 2-week window)
3. **Selected!** — Lottery result notification, ~1 in 3 odds
4. **LCA + Petition Filed** — DOL LCA (7 days) → I-129 to USCIS (Ellis: ~2 weeks total)
5. **USCIS Approval** — I-797 issued, 15 days (premium) or 3–4 months (standard)
6. **Consulate Interview** — DS-160, visa stamp at local U.S. embassy
7. **October 1st — You're in** — Work begins, 3-year H-1B status active
